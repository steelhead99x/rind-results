Why SHA-512 was 33× slower than ring on a microcontroller, and the fix that merged

On a Seeed XIAO ESP32-S3, HACL*'s unrolled SHA-2 code did not fit in the 16 KiB instruction cache. kixel-ai/rind#793 rolls SHA-512 and HChaCha20 on 32-bit microcontrollers and runs SHA-256 from internal RAM. What it fixed, what it costs, and which numbers are the fork's.

Plain words

  • A desktop CPU likes fully unrolled code: no loop overhead.
  • A small chip reads its code from flash through a small cache. When the code is bigger than the cache, it keeps missing and waits on flash.

Who is hit

  • SHA-512 and what is built on it, such as HMAC-SHA384 and HKDF-SHA384/512.
  • SHA-256 too, because both compete for the same 16 KiB.

The fix, three pieces · kixel-ai/rind#793

Roll the big loops on 32-bit microcontrollers, and run SHA-256 from internal RAM on ESP-IDF Xtensa

SHA-384 / SHA-512
  • A rolled 80-round loop over a 16-word window, low/generic/sha512.rs.
  • Only on 32-bit microcontrollers: target_os espidf or none. Hosted targets keep HACL*.
HChaCha20
  • Ten calls to one double round instead of the unrolled body, on 32-bit portable.
  • Same speed on i686 and about 5 KiB smaller.
SHA-256 on ESP-IDF Xtensa
  • rind's own FIPS 180-4 compression in .iram1, with K in .dram1.
  • Rotates and the block load are Xtensa asm!.
A new CI check, admin/esp32s3-sha256-layout, builds the body with esp rustc and checks placement, internal-RAM addresses, no call from the block, and the exact ssai sequences. A toolchain change that moves the code back to flash would otherwise fail silently.
